How levels are built. A parkour level is three ideas stacked together:

Platforms are the safe floors you land on (rooftops, logs, ice, sky islands).

Gaps are the empty air between platforms. If a gap looks wide, run first, then jump. A double jump or dash makes extra distance.

Timing is for things that move: sliding logs, fire jets, sinking pads, bouncing wind. Wait for the safe moment, then go.

Falling is just a cartoon poof. You pop back at the last checkpoint. No harm!

HOW IT'S BUILT

A few parkour ideas, said simply:

Platforms, gaps, timing. Every course is floors you can stand on, air you jump, and moving bits you wait for. Designers place a jump inside the cat's jump range so it is fair.

Moves are tools. Run then jump for distance. Double jump for extra height. Wall-run to keep going up a wall. Ledge grab saves a near-miss. Slide under beams. Dash for a burst. Roll when you land from high.

Checkpoints are kindness. A fall is a poof, not a game over. You pop back and try the same jump again.

Stars. Finish the level for 1. Be pretty fast for 2. Fast plus every golden fish for 3.

Saving is a superpower. Stars, fish, and outfits stay in a tiny notebook on this iPad (localStorage).

Play Together. A WebSocket room shares where each cat is so two screens match.
